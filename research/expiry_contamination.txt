  The crossed rows are the whole story. Held through expiry they pay
  when the stock FALLS -- 'always pays' means the median trade profits
  even on a wrong read, which no call can do. Cut at expiry they fall
  back in line with everything else, which is what a real contract does.

  774 of 3,388 trades (23%) held past an expiry. On those the
  median return falls from +91.6% to -99.1% once the contract is
  allowed to die on schedule, and the hold shortens from 125 bars
  to 74.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The shipped hurdle is 56.8%. It is wrong. Two independent corrections
  bracket the real one:

    drop the crossed trades      67.1%   every trade a clean 5-session hold
    cut every hold at expiry     68.9%   every trade kept, none mispriced

  Take the first. Truncation prices honestly but changes the question:
  a hold cut at expiry exits with no time value left, which is a
  different trade from the five-session hold being measured, and its
  -99.1% median is that difference rather than a result. Dropping the
  crossed trades costs 23% of the sample and biases toward entries
  earlier in the month, which is a real limitation and a smaller one.
  That the two agree within two points is what makes either usable.

  So the read must be right about 67% of the time, not 56.8%, against
  48.4% free. Required skill goes from 8.4 points above blind picking
  to roughly 19. And on the clean sample t(5d) is negative, so the
  trade is not merely harder than advertised -- at the measured
  accuracy it loses.

  Every downstream number priced on this cache inherits the same error:
  the strike ladder, the premium bands, and the elasticity gradient that
  looked like a finding an hour ago and was days-to-expiry in disguise.

  The stock-level shortlist is NOT affected -- 73% move 5%, 48.4% rise
  are measured on equity candles and never touch an option contract.
  What changes is the price of acting on it.
