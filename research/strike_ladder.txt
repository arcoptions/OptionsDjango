  'if right' is the median return when the stock did rise 5%; 'if wrong'
  when it did not. 'break-even' is the share of reads that must be right
  for the median trade to stop losing. Blind picking gives 48.4%.

  READ THE LAST THREE COLUMNS TOGETHER OR NOT AT ALL. The t columns test
  the MEAN, and the mean of an option trade is not the median: a handful
  of calls that run 5x drag it positive while most of the book bleeds. A
  positive t beside a negative median is a lottery, not an edge -- the
  same shape as the expiry-day gamma result.

  t(day) clusters same-morning entries, which fixes trades that share a
  market but NOT trades that share a HOLD. Every entry here is held five
  sessions and sampled daily, so each overlaps its four neighbours and
  t(day) is inflated by roughly sqrt(5). t(5d) re-runs it on entries
  spaced five sessions apart, which removes the overlap by construction
  at the cost of a fifth of the sample. t(5d) is the honest one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  No rung beats ATM. The cheaper contract's bigger multiple when
  right does not cover what it gives up when the move is slow or
  partial, so the shipped model keeps the ATM strike.
