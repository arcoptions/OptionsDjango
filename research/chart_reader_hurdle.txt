CAN THE REGIME BE CALLED IN ADVANCE? (breadth at the prior close)
============================================================================================================

  prior breadth  trades    break-even  median if PERFECT
  ----------------------------------------------------
  weakest         881         76.8%             +55.9%
  weak            895         71.1%             +54.6%
  ok              771         69.1%             +51.2%
  strongest       841         58.9%             +79.1%

  Monotone -- the hurdle falls at every step from weakest to
  strongest, so this IS a dose-response. It was not one before the
  expiry correction, and a dose-response is the check a lucky
  bucket usually fails. The top quartile:

    top quartile     841 trades over  43 days   median   -8.2%   day-t +1.27
    the rest       2,547 trades over 252 days   median  -36.6%   day-t -3.98

    split in half by date, top-quartile edge over the rest:
      first half   +27.0pp
      second half  +44.4pp

  This survives the two checks that killed it before the expiry
  correction: its day-t is now ABOVE the bucket it should beat, and
  the edge GROWS across the two halves instead of decaying. What
  still sinks it is the level, not the stability -- the best
  quartile needs 58.9% against the 48.4% a coin gives, so it makes
  the trade less bad rather than positive, on only 43 days.
  Either way the regime risk has to be borne, not timed -- which is the
  real reason this is a market bet.

============================================================================================================
DOES THE EXIT LOWER THE HURDLE? (no -- it breaks the measure)
============================================================================================================

  exit rule           break-even     median       mean     win%     day-t
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  hold to day 5            68.9%     -32.1%      47.7%    37.9%     -0.57
  first touch 1.5x         47.8%      -3.8%     -12.7%    49.3%    -11.00
  first touch 2x           57.4%     -20.3%      -2.1%    44.1%     -7.00
  first touch 3x           63.2%     -26.9%      10.8%    41.1%     -4.15

  Read the 1.5x row across: the hurdle falls to 47.8% against the 48.4% a
  coin gives, and 49.3% of trades finish positive -- yet its day-t is
  -11.00. The target caps every winner at +50% while the losers still run
  to -100%, so the COUNT of winners and the AVERAGE point opposite ways.
  Do not use the hurdle on a capped payoff: it is built on the
  probability of losing and cannot see a truncated right tail.

  And the hold column's own +47.7% mean does not survive either. It is
  a 5-day return sampled every day, so its windows overlap:

    naive day-clustered t   -0.57   (295 days)
    overlap-safe t          -0.84   (~59 independent 5-day blocks)
    top 1% of trades carry +44.9pp of that mean; the median is -32.1%
    and only 30.2% of days are positive.

  So no-skill call buying on the scan is NOT quietly profitable. The
  mean is pure payoff shape: the day-clustered t is already -0.57
  BEFORE any overlap correction, so the de-overlapping below is not
  even load-bearing. Agrees with the buying null outright.
